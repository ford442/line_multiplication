Line Multiplication

Multiply by drawing lines & counting dots

×

Add up the columns

Each column is one colour of dots. Count from the right, carry anything over 9.

How it works
  1. 1
    First number — each digit becomes a bundle of parallel lines. 23 → 2 lines, gap, 3 lines.
  2. 2
    Second number — draw it the same way, crossing the first.
  3. 3
    Count crossings — dots of the same colour sit in the same place value.
  4. 4
    Carry & read — add columns right to left, carrying tens, then read the answer.

Why does it work?

It's the distributive property, drawn. For 23 × 12:

(20 + 3) × (10 + 2)
= 200 + 40 + 30 + 6
= 2 hundreds + 7 tens + 6 ones = 276

When 2 lines cross 3 lines you get 2 × 3 = 6 crossings. Every bundle-meets-bundle region is one of those partial products, and its place value is the sum of the two digits' places.

Dot colours = place value

Did you know? Despite the nickname, this isn't how Japanese schools teach multiplication — it's a visual trick that went viral online, related to the "Chinese stick method".
